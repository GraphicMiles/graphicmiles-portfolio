Collections break when context lives everywhere.

Accounting systems record what is owed. They rarely show who promised to pay, what blocked delivery, or which follow-up should happen next.

Invoice status is fragmented

Balances sit in the ledger while payment promises, disputes, and delivery notes remain in spreadsheets, inboxes, and private messages.

Finance and sales chase different facts

Account owners know the customer history, but finance often sees only an overdue balance. The result is duplicated outreach and avoidable friction.

Incoming cash is difficult to explain

Without a shared follow-up history, forecasts depend on memory and status meetings instead of visible customer commitments.
