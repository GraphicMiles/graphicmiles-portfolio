Founder

"Same eye. Different room."

On connecting art, design, and software
The work

Eight years ago I walked into De Kings Eyes Clinic as the IT and Admin guy. No title that meant anything, just a computer and problems that needed solving. I'm the Operations & Systems Manager now. Six direct reports, three branches worth of operational architecture nobody assigned me to build, and a habit of getting handed increasingly ambiguous requests and just running with them.

Last month management asked for something to track glasses going to the lab and back, so delays show up before they become complaints. I spent three hours arguing with an AI model about the tech stack, it wanted Supabase and React, I wanted HTML and Firebase, and neither of us was winning. So I walked away and asked myself a different question: what does success actually look like here? Ten minutes later I had the data lifecycle drafted. Thirty minutes after that, the tracker was live.

You don't pay in code anymore. You pay in ambiguity.
Before any of that

Between 2020 and 2025, I was somewhere else entirely teaching myself anatomy, typography, motion design, color theory. The deliberate kind that takes years before it looks like anything. Bridgman, Loomis, Chris Do. I took clients and built Erudink Studios. I built a small reputation in an art community I still belong to.

I still think in that language. When Claude generated a schema for an ERP I was building, what came back was boxes, connected, structured, data flowing from one to the next, and it looked exactly like a typography layout. Like a floor plan. I'd seen that shape before. Just never in software.

Same eye. Different room.

The actual why

I kept forgetting what I'd actually done. Not because nothing happened, plenty happened. A patient's resignation letter answered in sixteen minutes, followed by a phone call that undid the coldness of it. A broken LAN cable debugged from first principles because the IT guy I used to be never actually left. A UI that looked like generic AI slop until I went back to Pinterest and fixed the taste problem an algorithm couldn't see.

None of it lived anywhere. No format had agreed to accept it as proof of anything. It just sat in me, until someone asked "what have you actually built" and I'd fumble the answer out of order, missing the best parts, because I was retrieving it from memory instead of from a record.

So I built the record.

React Native first. That didn't work. Two months away. Then HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Capacitor, Firebase, a working Android app on a device in under two weeks, built on a cheap phone in the gaps between a full-time job. I logged the entire build inside the app I was building. I didn't notice I was doing it until someone pointed it out.

Where it goes

I'm just the guy who got tired of forgetting. Bragsheet is what I built when that tiredness finally had somewhere to go, a private, permanent record of what you've actually done, captured as it happens, owned entirely by you, portable across every job and every reinvention you haven't had yet.

Your worth was never supposed to live inside your employer's HR system. It's supposed to live with you.
